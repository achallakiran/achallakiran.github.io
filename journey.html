An Origin Story

"BOT wasn't just a nickname. It was a prophecy."

Ravi Kiran Achalla  ·  Senior Engineering Manager II  ·  Walmart

scroll

Act I

POWER ON

Every origin story begins with a spark. Mine was a television screen in my cousin's living room. I was in Class 3 and nothing would ever be the same again.

Class 3. My cousin's house. I saw a video game for the first time. My jaw literally dropped. I couldn't stop thinking about how those characters moved, responded, and lived inside that screen.

I went home and negotiated the best deal of my young life with my dad. Rank #1 in class = 3 game cartridges. Rank #2 = 2. Anything else = nothing. We shook on it. Neither of us expected what came next.

Term after term, I topped my class. Not because of pressure — because I really, really wanted those games. The most effective study motivation ever invented.

But playing wasn't enough. I wanted to understand how things worked. So I started building — battery-operated cars from household junk, small robots from spare parts. The first engineer was awakening.

Act II

PLAYER 2 HAS ENTERED

College at KIIT was where everything changed. I arrived a crouched, slightly obese nerd in thick glasses. Freedom transformed me in ways I never expected.

My Counter Strike opponents called me BOT. I kept the name. It would prove more prophetic than anyone — including me — could have imagined.

IIT Kharagpur Tech Fest. Robotics on the podium. Design and poster competitions at IITs — won multiple times. Turns out the crouched nerd from school had serious range.

Guitars, posters, robots, code. Jamming with friends, designing college fest materials, building things. College was where all the pieces of me came together for the first time.

Act III

LOADING LEVEL 1

2009. First job at TCS. I had no idea I was about to fall in love with something that would define my entire career.

ETL, DataStage, data rivers flowing between systems — files to databases to XMLs, real-time and batch. Working for UK's largest apparel brand, Marks & Spencer. I didn't just move data. I fell obsessively in love with it.

First salary. First purchase: a Sony PS2 — still one of my most cherished possessions, with over 200 DVDs. Some priorities never change.

Ness Technologies, Bangalore. Great mentors, fast growth, and then — a flight to Canada. Small team, quick POC. We showed up, delivered, and came back with a contract to build a data warehouse for Canada's largest retail chain.

In my off hours, a friend and I launched Khanaman — an online food delivery startup in Ranchi. We were honestly ahead of our time. The food was great. The business ran for 2 years, then closed. Every engineer has a startup story.

Act IV

WELCOME TO WALMART

2015. I joined Walmart. I had never seen anything like it — geniuses everywhere, every technology available, and a scale that makes "large" sound small.

A floor full of masters — Oracle Exadata, Greenplum, DataStage, Ab Initio, even mainframes. You named a technology and it was there. I walked in wide-eyed and never looked back. The Enterprise Data Warehouse on Teradata was the centrepiece.

The Big Data wave arrived. Hadoop, MapReduce, then Spark and PySpark. I loved the raw power of crunching massive datasets. Built end-to-end automations that delivered massive effort reduction — and won the Walmart Innovator Award for it.

We built the Metrics Definition Tool — end-to-end from business metric definition to data layer to UI, cutting 4 months down to 2 weeks. The USPTO agreed it was worth protecting. Patent US11138241B2. That one felt earned.

The best part of growing wasn't the systems — it was watching engineers evolve from 3 to 8 to 20+ and then watching them become leaders themselves. Building people who build systems.

Act V

CLOUD WARS

The next frontier: move everything to GCP. At Walmart International scale — Canada, Mexico, China, Central America — that means everything, all at once.

3,000+ Hadoop ETL pipelines migrated to GCP using a custom-built automated conversion framework. Migration timeline cut by 60%. Zero production data lost. This is what platform engineering looks like at Walmart scale.

At this scale, every decision is measured in millions. AI-powered fraud detection blocking $200M annually. Real-time Kafka-driven inventory management maintaining 95% on-shelf availability across all markets. $5M saved through rigorous FinOps.

The eCommerce 360 Data Product — a single-tenant platform serving every Walmart International market. One source of truth for analytics, data science, and applications. 1,000+ daily users. $20M business impact. Zero silos. Full platformisation.

Act VI

BOT BECOMES REAL

Then in 2023, something arrived that gave me the exact same feeling as that television screen in my cousin's living room. Wide eyes. Everything changed. Again.

LLMs, multi-agent systems, Claude Code, Google ADK, Microsoft Copilot Studio — production agentic pipelines automating data ingestion, pipeline generation, quality validation, and support triage. 40% reduction in manual engineering effort. A kid who loved how game characters responded. Now building agents that respond, learn, and act autonomously. BOT was always the destination.
